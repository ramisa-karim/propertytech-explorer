An illustrative example of how tokenisation might represent units of notional property value.

Imagine a hypothetical commercial property valued at £1,000,000. For this simplified example, its notional value is divided into 10,000 tokens priced at £100 each.

Breaking down the example

£1,000,000 divided by 10,000 equal units gives a notional value of £100 per unit. For example, five units represent £500 of notional value in this simplified illustration.

In a real arrangement, tokens do not automatically confer legal ownership of part of a building. Actual rights, costs, risks and returns depend on the investment structure.

Educational example only. This is not a real investment opportunity. Token values do not guarantee returns, liquidity or legal ownership.
